# Betting at T-60 or 11:00 on the corrected forecast: closing-line value (research 105, 15 November 2026)

Question: research 104 found the price an hour before the off makes a
stronger forecast than the 11:00 price. Does betting at that price on the
corrected forecast's disagreements beat Betfair SP?

`research/t60_clv_105/run.py`. Forecast: the native 87-input forecast plus
the joint correction with the price group (`with-3600` or `with-1100`),
normalised within the race. Scored quarters 2025Q3–2026Q2. Retrospective:
the recipe and variants were chosen on these quarters; hold-out untouched.

- Taken price: last traded price at the cut, reduced for runners withdrawn
  after it (`rf_after_*`), capped at 51.
- Selection: `value_bet_selection` against the race-normalised share of the
  field at the cut.
- Settlement: exchange commission 2%; ROI interval resamples races.
- CLV: taken / BSP, geometric mean and median.

## Reading

- The corrected forecast with a morning price beats the close
  retrospectively, at both cuts and in every quarter.
- **For betting, 11:00 is better than T-60**, the opposite of the accuracy
  ranking. An earlier price leaves more of the day's money still to come,
  and the model leans the way that money moves.
- T-60's edge is about one tick: it disappears at one tick worse on all
  bets. The 11:00 edge survives two ticks at threshold 0.02.
- Not settled:
  - LTP is not a guaranteed fill, and 11:00 markets are thin, with wide
    spreads at long prices, where the ROI concentrates.
  - Recipe and variant were chosen on these quarters.

  A pre-registered single look at the hold-out, and live best-back prices
  (a forward paper record), are what would settle it.

## Does the corrected forecast add anything to BSP?

A two-weight blend, `a·log p_BSP + b·log p_model`, renormalised within the
race, fitted on earlier quarters and scored on the next (2025Q4–2026Q2,
8,232 races):

| Model in the blend | Weights (BSP, model) | Against BSP alone (1.74018) |
|---|---|---:|
| Native 87-input forecast | 0.95, 0.04–0.05 | −0.00009 |
| Corrected forecast (`more-groups-deeper`) | 0.94, 0.05–0.07 | −0.00044 [−0.00091, +0.00006], 3/3 |

The correction adds a sliver beyond the close: right sign in every quarter,
5x the native model's gain, still not significant and far too small to
clear commission. The 11:00 result above is anticipation of where the price
goes, not information the closing price lacks.

## Where the 11:00 edge comes from

The move from 11:00 to the off, `log(BSP share) − log(11:00 share)`,
regressed on each forecast's disagreement with the 11:00 share (95,913
runners):

| Forecast | Slope | Correlation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Native 87-input model, no price | 0.36 | 0.36 |
| Corrected, no price | 0.44 | 0.44 |
| Corrected with the 11:00 price inputs | 0.88 | 0.52 |

The model's fundamentals already forecast a third of the morning-to-off move.
The price inputs (trade age, changes, the 08:00 move, the gap) teach the
correction which 11:00 prices to trust, so its disagreement becomes a nearly
unbiased forecast of the move.

The bets (threshold 0.02) are not in stale or thin quotes. 94% are on runners
traded within 15 minutes of 11:00. The share selected rises with trading
activity (1% at ≤5 price changes, 15% at >60), and the most active group has
the best ROI (+7.8%, geo CLV 1.041). The dynamic threshold puts most bets at
short prices: 2,026 at odds 1–3 and 3,245 at 3–6. Stake capacity is not
measured: the timing table has price changes, not money matched.

## How much could be staked at 11:00

Joined to Betfair's public price files (frozen 6 November 2026), which give
money matched per runner in the morning (until about 11:00) and before the
off. 98% of runners join by market and selection ID; all 6,328 bets
(threshold 0.02, about one year) have volume.

| Money matched on the runner | p10 | p25 | Median | p75 | p90 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bets, morning | £361 | £779 | £1,636 | £3,453 | £6,971 |
| Bets, before the off | £19k | £31k | £55k | £99k | £155k |
| All runners, morning | £71 | £191 | £520 | £1,317 | £3,045 |

Bets fall on busier runners than average (median morning £3,673 at odds 1–3,
£1,391 at 3–6, £643 at 6–12). At the last traded price, per year:

| Stake per bet | Turnover | Profit | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2% of the morning's volume, at most £200 | £363k | £23k | +6.4% |
| 5% of the morning's volume, at most £200 | £681k | £47k | +6.9% |
| 10% of the morning's volume, at most £1,000 | £1.8m | £116k | +6.4% |

These are upper bounds:
- The morning's volume accumulates over hours, so the money offered at the
  best price at 11:00 is a fraction of it.
- The ROI is retrospective and at last traded price; one tick worse it is
  about 1.5 points lower.
- Bets placed this way would themselves move thin prices.

A median bet of 2% of its runner's morning volume is about £33.

| 6–7 | 1,371 | 823 | 1.030 | +13.3% [+0.6, +27.3] | 0.89 |

Bets beat the close in every band (geo CLV 1.009–1.034), with no trend in
hours. The median absolute move from 11:00 to the off is about the same
(0.21–0.23) whether the race is 2 or 8 hours away. The correction already
trusts the 11:00 price less for later races: the share of its disagreement
the market closes rises from 0.72 to about 0.90. ROI per band is noise.
