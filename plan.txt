Stage 1 — Is the info correct? (data integrity)
Today's pedigree-NaN and freq-restart bugs are unlikely to be the only ones.

Systematic leakage hunt: per-feature temporal audit (does any feature's value depend on the current race's result?), label-shuffle test (a leak-free model must collapse to ~market when labels are shuffled within race), and the cummax().shift(1) class of bugs (CLAUDE.md hard rule #1).
Label correctness: won/placed, non-runners, dead-heats, voids, places-paid — cross-checked against Betfair.
Coverage map: every feature's non-null rate and train-vs-live parity (the gap that broke live scoring) audited across the whole allowlist, not ad-hoc.
Deliverable: a data-integrity report; fix what's broken. Informs: which features can we even trust?
Stage 2 — Are we making the most of what we have?
Out-of-sample feature ROI: permutation/ablation under the locked WF. We carry ~150 allowlist features out of ~1100 — prune the dead weight and find what actually pays in CLV terms.
Under-exploited signal we already hold: sectional/pace data (RTV, partial), and market microstructure — we have pre-off LTP and BSP, so the shape of the price move and money flow are features we're barely using.
Deliverable: a leaner, higher-signal feature set + 1–2 new microstructure features, each justified by WF CLV.
Stage 3 — Are we using the right model?
This is likely the biggest lever, and it's structural, not cosmetic:

Respect the race structure. We currently train a per-horse binary classifier and normalize — which ignores that exactly one horse wins. The field-standard is a conditional logit / softmax over the field (Harville/Henery) or learning-to-rank (LambdaMART, native in LightGBM). Switching to race-aware modeling typically improves discrimination materially.
Place probs derived from win probs (Harville) instead of a separate place classifier — more coherent and less to break.
The Benter two-stage: the market price is the single strongest predictor; the right design is a fundamental model whose job is to estimate the market's residual error, combined with the BSP-implied prob in a second stage. We tried a crude "market anchor" and it looked optimistic — do it properly and validate at BSP/CLV.
Specialized sub-models (flat vs jumps, distance bands) + a stacked meta-model.
Deliverable: race-aware base model + market-combination layer, A/B'd against the current one on WF CLV.
Stage 4 — New public/cheap data sources (prioritized by signal × availability × effort)
High value: sectional/GPS times (Total Performance Data / RTV — expand coverage), declared wind-surgery & first-time gear (UK publishes these; partly have gear), horse bodyweight where published, official going-stick readings.
Medium: trainer/yard form streaks & seasonality, sales/pedigree prices, gallop/workout reports, distance-and-class move patterns.
Approach: add one source at a time, each justified by WF CLV before the next.
Deliverable: ranked data-source shortlist with a spike on the top one.
Stage 5 — Combine / add models
Stack fundamental + market + sub-models into a calibrated meta-model; isotonic calibration per race-type. (Neural tabular models are an option but GBMs usually win here — only if Stage 3 plateaus.)

do we make use of finishing times?

Yes — quite a few, and this session's hard lesson sharpens how to pick them. The bar isn't "is it predictive?" — it's "is it orthogonal to what the BSP already prices?" Stewards data failed because it was redundant with form. So rank candidates by how little the market already captures them.

Tier 1 — you already hold the data, barely using it
1. Market microstructure (highest expected value). Your own plan.txt says you're "barely using" pre-off LTP + BSP. This is the single least-redundant signal because it is the market's own late information:

Price-move shape: steam vs drift, magnitude, and when it happened. A horse backed from 8s into 5s is a different proposition than a 5/1 that drifted.
Late money (last 5-10 min) — the closest thing to an insider/sharp-money signal. Often the most predictive variable available.
Matched-volume profile, back/lay imbalance, price volatility, BSP-vs-bookmaker-SP divergence. This is free, you have it, and it's where actual sharp edges live. I'd put this above sectionals.
2. Race-level pace projection. You have per-horse pace_style features but the lever is the race-shape model: count front-runners, project the pace (lone-leader steal vs collapse), and score each horse's fit to the likely scenario + draw. Pace fit is technical enough that the market prices it imperfectly.

3. Sectional-derived "trouble" — a data-native replacement for the stewards text. Your RTV/ATR sectionals already give finishing-speed % and energy distribution. Horses closing fastest at the line but beaten ("staying on", trip too short, traffic) are the clean, complete version of the stewards "unlucky" signal — no text parsing, full coverage, and it captures cases the stewards never reported. This is the better way to do what stewards data couldn't.

Tier 2 — new sources, genuinely orthogonal
4. Par-time / going-corrected speed figures. Use the day's actual times vs historical par for the going to derive a "true going" and properly calibrate speed figures — sharper than the official going word, and readable pre-off from the early races on the card. This makes every existing speed feature better rather than adding a redundant one.

5. Pedigree aptitude, applied only where form is thin. You have pedigree, but the value is conditional: sire/damsire going and distance aptitude for debutants, first-time-soft, and horses trying a new trip — exactly the spots where the market has least to go on. Pedigree is noise for exposed horses and signal for unexposed ones; gate it that way.

6. Bodyweight + weight change. Declared in Ireland/HK (and increasingly UK). Big swings vs last run are a genuine physical-condition signal the market reacts to slowly.

Tier 3 — connections / intent
7. Jockey-booking signals. Which horse gets the stable jockey when a yard runs several; a top jockey booked for an outsider. Cheap to derive from declarations, and a real intent proxy.

8. Layoff/comeback profiles. Trainer strike rate off specific break lengths (60-90 days, wind-op return, etc.) — more specific than generic trainer form.

The discipline that makes any of these pay
Given how sharp BSP is, evaluate every candidate the Benter way — does it predict the market's residual error, not the raw outcome — under your locked walk-forward at CLV, one source at a time. Most will be redundant (as stewards was). My ranked bet on what actually clears that bar: microstructure first, sectional-derived pace/trouble second, par-time going-calibration third. Those three are the most orthogonal to the form the market already knows, and two of the three use data you already have.

The sharpest insight from this session to aim at: the market beats us specifically on debutants and favourites because of private stable information (gallops, fitness, intent). Data that proxies that private info is worth more than anything that refines public form.

Tier 1 — genuinely orthogonal, obtainable
1. GPS / stride biomechanics (beyond sectionals). Sectional times tell you pace; stride length × frequency and energy distribution tell you how a horse produces speed and whether it was idling, hampered, or all-out. Much harder for the market to price than split times.

Total Performance Data (totalperformancedata.com) — the official GPS/tracking provider for a large set of GB tracks; stride length, stride frequency, position-in-running, speed-by-furlong. Commercial license. This is the gold standard and complements whatever sectional source you're adding.
2. Betfair traded volume / money-flow (market microstructure). This is the #1 untapped lever we already flagged. You have the price trajectory (ltp_300s/60s/last/bsp) but no traded volume or ladder depth. Volume-weighted moves and where money enters (which side of the book, how late) are the most direct proxy for the private info that beats us — and it's a latency/processing edge, not a form edge.

Betfair historic data (historicdata.betfair.com) — the PRO files include matched volume per runner over time, not just LTP. Live: the Exchange Stream API ladder.
3. Wind surgery / first-time gelding / surgery notifications. Breathing operations are a strong, documented signal and the market systematically underweights second/third-time wind ops. First-time gelding too. This attacks "last run understates the horse."

BHA publishes first-time wind-surgery declarations; gelding/headgear is in the racecard feed. You're already scraping BHA stewards — same infrastructure.
4. Auction / sale price. This is the one that directly attacks the debutant gap where we lose to the market. A horse's yearling/breeze-up price is a public proxy for the private expectation the market is pricing on debut.

Tattersalls and Goffs publish sale results; Racing Post aggregates sales data. Join on horse_id.
Tier 2 — worth adding, more incremental
5. Stewards "trouble-in-running" / excuses — you're already collecting BHA + IHRB stewards reports. Combined with the new sectionals this becomes powerful: a horse with a slow split AND a "hampered/short of room" steward note ran better than its result. Build the join — that's a force-multiplier on data you already have.

6. GoingStick numeric readings — the official penetrometer number (e.g. 7.2), not the verbal "good to soft." Published per GB meeting, rarely used quantitatively. Sharper ground signal than the going category you currently one-hot.

One honest caveat on jurisdiction
The single most valuable biometric — horse bodyweight and run-to-run weight change — is routinely published in Hong Kong / Australia / Japan, but not in GB/IRE. So it's only on the table if you ever expand jurisdictions; don't go hunting for it in British/Irish data.
